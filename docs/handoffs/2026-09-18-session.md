## What was found and fixed

1. **The aim-down-sights vignette, never seen since it was built on
   12 September.** Two causes, both now measured in the game. The sprite
   lives in a corner of the flat texture and was painted only when its
   strength changed, which writes **one** swapchain image; the run reports
   `images=3`, so at best one frame in three could ever have shown it, and
   the others held whatever the capture last put there. And the quad was a
   fixed 3.6 m at 1 m, whose darkening peaks 61 degrees off the view's centre
   while Virtual Desktop shows about 52: at 45 degrees it reached 9 of 255.
   It is now painted per image and sized from the runtime's own field of
   view (`peak_deg=52.1` in the log).
2. **An aim zoom** (the user asked for "a small zoom to ADS - maybe 10-15%"):
   "Aim zoom (%)", default 12, range 0 to 30. The mod already owns the
   rendered frustum, so aiming divides its tangents by the magnification
   while the viewer keeps submitting the runtime's field of view. An eye
   readback with the zoom active shows the world correctly projected, which
   was the one real risk.
3. **The item radial's last two faults**: it owned the stick from the frame
   *after* its control went down, and reach-to-interact was dead whenever a
   gun was out, both through a rule that gave way to two-hand support's
   standing offer. One shared rule now, with `exclusive_stick` carried on
   the request so the bindings silence the stick from the press itself.
4. **A review caught four more**, including one introduced the same hour:
   reach would have cancelled its own grip one frame after taking it, and
   the vignette could have been submitted where its sprite is never painted
   (opaque captured pixels across the whole view when the reticle is off).
5. **The streaming rate is the largest performance lever found so far.**
   See below.
6. Smaller: a null-swapchain layer guard; the near-eye scan now takes the
   real camera (it had never hidden a mesh in 21 runs, and still hides none,
   so its criterion is the open question); a body copy whose rig does not
   match is hidden rather than left standing in the player's view; the
   haptics wrapper is variadic like the profiler's.

## The streaming rate (the user's question, answered in part)

Same Hub, same 90 per cent FOV tangent, same flags; only Virtual Desktop's
streaming rate differs (the viewer records it as `last_display_period_ms`):

| arm | rate | GPU per pair | game pairs a second |
|---|---|---|---|
| hub-fov90-1, -2 (16 Sept) | 120 Hz | 21.2, 20.7 ms | 59.2, 58.5 |
| hub-100hz-1 (18 Sept) | 100 Hz | 19.6 ms | **72.3** |

A sixth off the streaming rate bought about 22 per cent more rendered
frames. The rate cannot be set from the PC (not in the streamer's registry,
not in `%APPDATA%\Virtual Desktop`, and the Quest exposes no
`debug.oculus.refreshRate`), so 90, 80 and 72 Hz need either the user or the
Virtual Desktop app driven over adb.

## Things worth carrying forward

- **Instrument before launching.** One run answered five questions because
  the log lines were added first: the two pixel spaces markers use
  (`back_buffer` equals `RESOLUTION_LOOKUP` in the headset, so the marker
  size has no window dependence, and `scale` is exactly 1908/1920), the
  worst offset a routed marker draw asks for (72 px against a 512 half
  cell, so the pickup sliver is **not** content overflow), the skull feed,
  the body's neck geometry and the vignette's own geometry.
- **A feature that cannot be driven unattended will not be proven.** The
  vignette went five days unseen because nothing could press the aim. A
  three-line test flag (`darktidevr_ads_test.flag`) fixed that permanently.
- **The two readbacks are not interchangeable.** The *projected*-eye
  readback is only copied on a frame that draws the tracked cuffs or a
  board, so a gameplay run consumes the request and writes nothing; the
  *shared*-eye readback is the game's own pair and always works. The runner
  now asks for both.
- **The body overlay's uniform scale is at its cap** (1.3) and the arms with
  it: in the first run the arm solve wanted a target 0.759 m from the
  shoulder against a 0.709 m reach, so the soft stretch ran on every one of
  25200 frames. That is the argument for the arm-length work, in numbers.

## Later the same day

After the four runs, the list kept moving:

- **A correction to my own conclusion.** The first reading of the arm-length
  A/B said the calibration's span was too short to use. Checked against the
  saved calibration it is not: 1.559 m against a predicted 1.630 is 1.6
  standard deviations, inside what the derivation accepts, and the 0.518 m
  arm it derives is plausible for a 1.78 m player. What conflicts is the
  uniform scale. A new dev-flag mode, `overlaytrue`, drops the scale, bends
  the spine and applies the calibrated arms: the worst soft stretch falls
  from 0.42 m to 0.03 m and the shoulder-to-hand distance from 0.82 m to
  0.59 m, while 18 cm of neck-to-head gap remain. Neither version is
  finished; checklist item 59 asks the user which reads better, and that
  answer decides whether the legs are next.
- **Robustness**: three hand displays no longer switch off for a whole
  session on one transient error (and the first attempt at that was hollow —
  a review caught that `destroy()` never reset the count and the module is
  installed once per session); the crosshair scale no longer retries a failed
  file write every frame; a full marker atlas says so once.
- **Teammate panels are keyed by the player**, not by their place in an
  unordered loop, so a join or a respawn cannot draw one teammate's bars over
  another's head for a frame.
- **The hand overlay's and the marker atlas's draws are inside the profiler**
  at last; every profile taken until now was blind to the whole atlas pass.
- **A test that measures every hand display against its cell** at the 100, 90
  and 80 per cent FOV tangents, with a self-check that it can still see the
  spill it was written for. It says the wrist display clears its cell by a
  pixel (its fit working), the radial keeps full-size labels at 90 and
  shrinks two of them at 80, and the rest are nowhere near their edges.
- **The reticle plan is written down** in the detailed list: the route (draw
  into the eye images beside the tracked cuffs, not a second projection
  layer), the four things it needs, the gate and how a readback proves it.

## The last stretch: the reticle, and four reviews

After the handover above was written the day kept going, and the shape of it
changed: every piece below was reviewed by a subagent before it was allowed
near the game, and every review found something real.

- **The projection anything drawn into the eye images must use.** The images
  are submitted with a recentered symmetric projection and the game renders
  the pair with the same one; the tracked cuffs were drawn into them with the
  runtime's own off-axis frustum. At Virtual Desktop's field of view that is
  6.2 degrees horizontally and 5.9 vertically, and the horizontal error
  reverses between the eyes -- a stereo cue claiming the wrong depth. The cuff
  overlay is a development switch, which is why it was never worn. Computed
  once now, before the theatre command list, and read by the cuffs, the
  reticle and the layer alike; `core_math` pins both numbers.
- **The reticle out of Virtual Desktop's quad layer**, the last piece of the
  FOV-tangent work, built as the detailed list planned it and gated off
  (`darktidevr_reticle_in_eyes.flag`, or `-ReticleInEyes`). The gate is a flag
  file rather than an environment variable because an unattended run starts
  the game through Steam, so the viewer never sees the runner's environment --
  the switch would have worked only with a head in the headset, which is
  exactly the trap the vignette spent five days in.
- **The aim zoom was moving the world and not the reticle.** A day-old
  regression in the shipped path, found by the review of the drawn reticle.
- Two of A8's smaller items: a panel looked at from straight above holds its
  roll instead of spinning, and text is guarded top and bottom as well as left
  and right.

**A rule I invented cost the afternoon's run.** I had widened "stop launching
once the user is home" to "stop as soon as any message arrives", and the user
sent two feature requests from work. They removed it: *"only hold off on
launches if I state I'm home and testing"*. A safety rule that fires on the
wrong signal is not a cautious version of the right rule, it is a different
and worse rule -- this one spent a day of available launch time to protect
against nothing. The run is described in
[the results](../phase1/unattended-results-2026-09-18.md); it is the first
thing to do next, and it is worth doing before the user is asked to wear
anything, because the reticle draws into the world image and a mistake there
is visible corruption rather than a missing overlay.

**New in the list, both from the user**: nested submenus for the mod options
(DMF does support them -- groups nest with no depth limit and the view indents
by depth) with a reorganisation of the segments either way; and what the rest
of the industry does about scaling body height and arm length independently,
which is today's body A/B seen from the other end.

| a hidden keybind | DMF hides a checkbox's children; the mirror works without its parent |

The last two are the ones to remember. Both were **fixes that were worse than
the bug** for part of the input range, and neither would have shown up in a
single worn session -- the zoom one is correct along the recentre direction
and wrong everywhere else, and the keybind one only bites the default
configuration.

Two tests came out of it that did not exist before and would have caught
their own class: `test-options-data.lua` checks the options tree against
DMF's own `validate_*_data` rather than against what its author thought of,
and refuses any nesting whose justification is not written down; and
`core_math` pins the exact displacement between the two projection
conventions, so they cannot be swapped again.

**The one thing still owed is the run.** Nothing built today has been in the
game. The reticle draws into the world image, so it wants the unattended
proof before it wants a head.
