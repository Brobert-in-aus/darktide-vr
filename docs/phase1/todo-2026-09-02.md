### 2026-09-02 clustered-light investigation and candidate correction

The Penances-light defect is an eye-dependent local-light admission problem,
not a shadow-camera problem. Controlled worn tests established that coincident
eye orientation removes the asymmetry, while zero IPD does not. A 20% wider
engine visibility FOV admits the missing light in both eyes, but applying that
alone makes other lighting incorrect near the eye edges.

Narrow shader and binding traces identified the exact path:

- local-light raster VS/PS hashes are `5c6cd369626f261a` and
  `be559cb63c32aa02`;
- root CBVs 0 and 2 contain the same 84-byte raster constants;
- the stock projection and aspect stay unchanged under visibility padding;
- the separate FOV scalar at byte offset 72 changes from `1.72788` to
  `1.90448`;
- the cluster pixel shader consumes that widened scalar for ray
  reconstruction, but does not consume the compensating post-projection
  transform. This is the measured cause of the edge-lighting artifacts.

The production candidate therefore keeps the wider engine frustum only for
CPU light admission and restores the original runtime FOV scalar in those
exact two shader-bound constant buffers before Stingray uploads them. It is
paired and fail-safe: Lua widens visibility only when the native, signature-
checked upload correction reports active. A missing hook leaves the established
projection unchanged. Normal sync enables
`darktidevr_cluster_light_visibility_fix.flag` and removes the diagnostic
`darktidevr_cluster_trace.flag`.

The production run on 2026-09-02 reached nonzero `shared_ready`, sustained
roughly 58-60 fresh stereo pairs/s after loading, and applied 29,502/29,502
eligible constant corrections with zero root misses, resource misses, queue
rejects, Lua errors or engine crashes during the measured interval. This proves
the narrow mechanism and lifecycle, but **does not replace worn visual
acceptance**. On return, reproduce the Penances view and check both that the
original asymmetric light is gone and that the broad eye-edge artifacts from
raw `VisibilityPadding` are absent.

### 2026-09-02 final production performance profile

The final 240-second VDXR run used the actual `2496x2688` per-eye extent,
entered Psykhanium from a closed game and exercised the production path with a
synthetic head sweep. Once stereo attached, OpenXR submissions and fresh pairs
were the same population, commonly about 58-62 Hz. This establishes that the
roughly 115 fps Steam/frame-generation display is not 115 unique stereo pairs
delivered by the bridge.

The opt-in GPU profiler collected 57 warmed windows. Its eye intervals averaged
9.331 ms left and 5.849 ms right, with a 15.180 ms reported interval sum. The
expensive post-boundary work moved between eyes as the synthetic view turned;
there is no permanently slow physical eye. These queue intervals can overlap,
so their sum is not end-to-end pair latency. The safe conclusions are that Lua
submission is negligible, the bottleneck is downstream on the GPU queue and the
second view still contributes material work.

The run finished 17,755/17,755 OpenXR submissions, 10,258 fresh pairs, 7,477
loading/fallback frames, zero reused shared frames and one startup pose mismatch.
The clustered-light correction reached 110,818/110,818 patches with zero
rejects/root misses/resource misses. No mod error, DRED/device-removal signature
or engine crash occurred, and all run-scoped flags and processes were cleaned
up.
